\begin{abstract}
Scientific evidence pipelines increasingly rely on content-addressed custody and hash-valid artifacts, yet syntactically valid objects can remain semantically inconsistent. We present an evidence-pipeline reliability study centered on explicit terminal states, row-level accounting invariants, and closed-vocabulary identity guards. Audits, deterministic ablations, and retrospective successor evaluations show that (i) a historical aggregate reported zero abstentions while row-level data contained three; (ii) a successor implementation with derived aggregates restores invariant consistency; (iii) a deterministic identity contract passes under synthetic fixtures but a single observed-source pair admits downstream passage when a guard is bypassed; and (iv) the historical G1/G2 corpora were not recovered from admitted provenance, while contemporary successor evaluations were run on admitted subsets. The morphology null benchmark was independently reproduced and retained as negative evidence. We do not claim therapeutic efficacy, clinical utility, biological rescue, population-level biomedical error rates, treatment recommendations, or real-world evidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Machine-learning systems for biomedical hypothesis generation must separate \emph{what was computed} from \emph{what may be claimed}. Content-addressed storage and hash verification establish integrity of bytes, not meaning: aggregates can disagree with row ledgers, evaluation wiring can fail to exercise intended disagreements, and missing corpora can leave historical numbers unverified. We frame this work as an evidence-pipeline control problem: normalize, validate, admit or abstain, derive results, and attach claim ceilings before projection to manuscripts \cite{prov_dm,stodden2016}.

\section{Related Work and Terminology}
Selective prediction and reject options allow models to abstain under uncertainty \cite{chow1970,geifman2017}. Workflow and data provenance standards document lineage without guaranteeing semantic correctness \cite{prov_dm}. Biomedical identifier normalization relies on authorities such as HGNC and UniProt \cite{hgnc2021,uniprot2023}. Reproducibility guidance distinguishes replication from reproducibility and encourages reporting null results \cite{nas2019}. Regulatory Real-World Evidence frameworks define boundaries we do not claim here \cite{fda_rwe}.

\textbf{GAP accounting.} Historical summary reported zero abstentions; row-level candidates contained three \textsc{Abstain} terminals. Successor run: $N_{\text{input}}{=}4$, $N_{\text{admitted}}{=}1$, $N_{\text{abstained}}{=}3$, summary abstain count $=3$; invariants pass.

\textbf{Identity guard.} Lane~A: deterministic closed-vocabulary contract passes. Lane~B: one observed source/canonical pair (\texttt{TAZ/cardiolipin module} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{TAZ}) yields \textsc{Abstain} under guard and downstream admission under bypass (\emph{unsupported identity admission}); not a population error rate. Historical evaluation invoked \texttt{reconcile(symbol, symbol)}; regression confirms the wiring defect.

\textbf{G1 successor evaluation.} On the admitted contemporary ClinVar subset ($N{=}364$), a retrospective successor sequence guard emitted 98 records and abstained 266 ($364{=}98{+}266$): frameshift 131, no protein notation 118, residue mismatch 16, position out of range 1. These abstention categories are accounting terminals in the contemporary successor evaluation, not a population biomedical error rate and not proof that source records are globally incorrect. This is \emph{not} exact historical reproduction.

\textbf{G2 successor evaluation.} Contemporary fetch accounting: 746 IDs fetched, 364 kept, 382 excluded as large/multi-gene copy-number events, 0 without summary ($746{=}364{+}382{+}0$). The 382 exclusions reflect unsupported gene-specific attribution under the frozen G2 predicate, not a separately validated ``silent error'' rate. A historical 100-ID ESummary failure ($\sim$16.5\,MB XML vs.\ 10\,MB JSON ceiling) is recovered via split-and-retry; the contemporary 746-row corpus does not reproduce a historical 742-row corpus.

\textbf{Provenance recovery.} Admitted external bootstrap package (206\,MB, SHA-256 verified) does not contain recoverable historical G1/G2 derivation chains; numeric substring matches in unrelated CSV fields are rejected as evidence.

\textbf{Null benchmark.} The morphology null benchmark was independently reproduced: known-pair rank 28/50, reciprocal-rank enrichment vs.\ shuffle $<1$; the negative result was retained and not suppressed.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Scope is intentionally small: $N{=}1$ observed-source ablation; the historical G1/G2 corpora were not recovered from admitted provenance (contemporary successor subsets were evaluated separately); same designers performed parts of evaluation; no therapeutic efficacy, biological rescue, clinical utility, treatment recommendations, observed-source evaluations framed as RWE, or population prevalence claims; EXP-005 replication not executed pending corpus freeze; infrastructure integrity does not establish semantic correctness. No structure-prediction execution is part of admitted experiment evidence.

\section{Reproducibility and Provenance}
Artifacts, preregistrations, and SHA-256 manifests reside in the project Frozen Custody Graph. Anonymous supplementary materials omit identifying metadata. Historical unverified counts remain quarantined as \texttt{UNVERIFIED\_HISTORICAL\_CLAIM}. The submitted PDF and anonymous supplementary artifacts are content-addressed as immutable evidence objects: each payload leaf is identified by SHA-256 and included in a canonical manifest; verification recomputes leaf and manifest hashes offline. This establishes byte identity and provenance closure, not biological correctness, therapeutic efficacy, or correctness of every scientific inference.

\section{Conclusion}
Hash-valid custody is necessary but insufficient for trustworthy evidence pipelines. Explicit terminal states and invariants surface failure modes that remain syntactically admissible---a prerequisite before any biomedical performance claim.
